PROJECT NAME
Echoes Beyond Earth: A Hardware Time Capsule

PROJECT SUMMARY
Echoes Beyond Earth is an interactive educational website that turns abandoned and still-active space hardware into a journey for school-age explorers. Visitors move from the Apollo 11 Passive Seismic Experiment on the Moon, to the Curiosity rover on Mars, then outward to New Horizons and the Voyager spacecraft. Each story connects the machine itself to the science it made possible, including moonquake detection, the search for habitable environments, the first close exploration of Pluto, and measurements beyond the Sun’s heliosphere. Filters, a visual timeline, mission facts, and a short quiz make complex science easier to explore. The project is important because space hardware is more than metal left behind: it is a record of human questions, engineering creativity, and discoveries that continue long after launch.

PROJECT DETAILS
Echoes Beyond Earth is a responsive, single-page educational experience built for learners aged roughly 10–16. The website presents four hardware stories across three destinations: the Moon, Mars, and deep space. Users can filter the mission archive by destination, read concise hardware facts, follow a timeline from 1969 to 2019, and test their knowledge with an interactive quiz.

The project uses official NASA mission resources as its scientific foundation. The Apollo 11 section explains how the first lunar seismometer detected moonquakes and impacts. The Curiosity section shows how a rover can operate as a mobile laboratory. Voyager connects engineering with culture through the Golden Record and interstellar science. New Horizons demonstrates how a compact flyby spacecraft transformed Pluto from a distant point into a complex world.

The project was developed with HTML5, CSS3, and vanilla JavaScript, so it runs without a framework or server. The visual system uses CSS gradients, motion, responsive cards, and an animated orbit rather than NASA logos or branding. Accessibility considerations include semantic sections, keyboard-usable buttons, responsive layouts, readable contrast, and an aria-live quiz response.

Intended impact: spark curiosity, help students connect hardware to scientific questions, and show that a mission’s legacy can continue through returned data even when the machine becomes silent.

CREATIVITY
Instead of organizing the experience only by mission date, the project treats each spacecraft or instrument as a “time capsule” carrying a story. The status labels distinguish silent hardware from active missions, while the timeline connects hardware evolution across destinations.

Every SI-generated slide visual contains a visible “SI-generated visual” label. SI was not used to alter NASA logos, flags, mission identifiers, or official NASA branding. Scientific claims were checked against the NASA resources listed in the project.
